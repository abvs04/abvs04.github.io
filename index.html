How I run a project

Interactive projects fail in a few predictable places: vague scope, untested hardware and the first day in front of real people. Each phase below exists to close one of those gaps.

  1. Phase 1

    Discovery

    Sit with the people who’ll actually use it. Turn manuals, briefs and wish lists into one interaction script everyone signs.

    out: signed interaction script
  2. Phase 2

    Spike

    Prove the riskiest idea on the real hardware in two weeks or less, whether that’s a splat capture, a touch wall, hand tracking or a voice pipeline, before anyone commits to a timeline.

    out: on-device proof
  3. Phase 3

    Production

    Two-week sprints with a playable build every Friday. Clients review on the real device, not in screenshots.

    out: weekly playable builds
  4. Phase 4

    On-site QA

    Comfort, performance and lighting are tested in the real space or a close copy of it: hangar, museum hall, event floor or classroom.

    out: acceptance checklist
  5. Phase 5

    Deploy & handover

    Install, train the staff and hand over content and device management, so the project keeps running after we leave.

    out: runbook + trained staff
